What this tool does: Under the Environment Act 2021, all storm overflows impacting designated bathing waters must be improved by 2035, with all remaining overflows addressed by 2050. This tracker helps the public see which sites have recorded overflow events and whether contracts have been publicly confirmed to address them.
Enforcement history

In 2021, Southern Water pleaded guilty to 6,971 unpermitted discharges between 2010 and 2015, resulting in a £90 million fine: the largest ever imposed on a UK water company. In 2019, the company was ordered to pay a £126 million package (£3m fine plus £123m in customer rebates) for failing to operate treatment sites properly and intentionally misreporting performance data to the regulator.

How this was built

This tool was built entirely from public data: the Storm Overflow Portal (real-time overflow events), Find a Tender (UK government procurement notices), the Environment Agency's EDM annual returns, Ofwat's Annual Performance Reports, and press releases from Southern Water and its contractors.

The methodology connects environmental performance data to procurement records for the first time in one place. Every contract value shown links to its source document. Where data could not be verified, it is marked as such.

Absence of a publicly verified contract does not necessarily mean no work is planned. Southern Water may use internal delivery, framework call-offs, or confidential commercial agreements not captured in public procurement notices.

Corrections and updates welcome. This tracker uses only publicly available data. We welcome corrections, additional contract information, or context from Southern Water or its contractors. Please contact nelith.bandularatne@gmail.com to update any record.
